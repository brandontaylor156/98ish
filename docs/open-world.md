# Roam: the open world (Explore Valencia, Simi Valley, Northridge, Newport Beach)

Built 2026-10-09, phase 1 of the owner's open-world ask: "At Paseo I'd like to actually map out all of Valencia so me and my girlfriend can explore", "run around, get in random cars, explore with her", "Easter eggs scattered throughout the city". Lives inside Pickleball 98 (the pickleball rule) as **Explore Valencia** from My Park's Paseo Club. Help topic `explore-town`. Not yet tried on a real iPhone.

2026-10-09 (later): the owner's "do the same thing for Simi Valley, for Northridge, for Newport Beach" (NYC, Chicago and Lake Tahoe come later): three more towns, each entered from its venue, Newport Beach with its ocean, harbour and piers, and **Go to...** between towns (riders come along). See "The towns", "The coast" and "Going to another town" below.

## Architecture
- **Its own module:** `client/src/roam/`. Nothing in it imports 98ish except **one adapter, `host98.js`**. Everything else (tiles, rendering, walking, cars, sync, eggs, the HUD and its CSS) is plain three.js + React with no Win98 UI library (the HUD draws its own grey bevels in `ui/roam.css`). A standalone app later = a new host file (a figure maker, a sky, a store, a socket) + its own render loop; the rest moves as is.
- **The host** (`makeHost98`): `figure(look, { lite })` (Pickleball's athletes animated by `anim.js` walking, lifted to the ground's height; `lite` = no motion matching, for people walking by), `npcLook(id)` (a regular's look, seeded), `sky.create/look` (My Park's Real Sky: `realsky.js`, `sky.js realLook`, Open-Meteo weather), `environment()` (the venues' CC0 outdoor HDRI for car paint and glass), `surface(kind)` (the venues' CC0 surface textures as shared uniforms, `park/surfaces.js surfaceUniform`), `trees()` (the venues' tree kit, `park/detail.js treeKit`), `audio` (`horn(on)`, `radio(on)`: My Park's lo-fi loop), `store` (per-user `98ish.` localStorage), `fetch`, `anisotropy`. On Low the surface textures and trees are left out (the plain look). Hooks: `useRoamNet` (joins `server/roam` over the shared connection), `useRoamVoice` (the spatial voice of My Park, `utils/voice`).
- **The world** (`world.js createRoam({ town, host, phone, quality, start, labelsEl, onHud, onEvent })`) speaks Pickleball's engine-world interface (`scene, camera, frame(dt), resize, key, setStick, drag, clearKeys, refigure, dispose`, plus `setSprint`, `setDrive`, `action`), so `engine.setWorld(world)` draws it with the engine's renderer, frame clock and dynamic resolution. Events out: `found`, `car`, `ride`, `venue` (Back to the courts), `toast`, `worldMenu`.
- **In Pickleball.jsx:** `startRoam(town, { from })` (disposes the park world, you come out where you stood; `ensureOpen` moves you out of any wall), `leaveRoam`, `RoamHud`/`RoamMenu`/`RoamFound`, the park chip `.pkExploreChip` (within ~28 m of the town's `venues.paseo.back` spot) and the park menu's **Explore Valencia...** (`ParkMenu explore`). Dev hooks: `window.__roam` (the world: `info`, `teleport`, `setHour`, `findRoad`, `nearestRoadPoint`, `clearPath`, `parkedNear`, `eggs`), `window.__pbStartRoam(town, opts)`.

## Data pipeline
- **Tiles:** Web Mercator z16 (~504 m at Valencia). `data/tile.js buildTile` turns Overpass elements (`data/osm.js townQuery` + `compactElement`) and an elevation sampler into one compact JSON tile: names table, a 33x33 height lattice (dm, delta-coded rows; edges shared with neighbours), roads `[class, width dm, flags, name, layer, ...line]`, bridge decks `k` (heights along each bridge from the ground at the whole bridge's two ends; footbridges arch up to 5.5 m), buildings `[kind, height dm, min, levels, roof, name, ...ring]` (in the tile of their middle), areas `[class, ...ring]` (clipped), mapped trees and tree rows, named places, and `s` (street nodes: `[kind, u, v, ...]`, `STREET` lamp / signals / stop from `highway=street_lamp|traffic_signals|stop`; only when the tile has any). Coordinates are 4096 units a tile (~12 cm), delta-coded; lines and areas clipped with a 12 m margin; Douglas-Peucker 2-3 units.
- **Classes and defaults** (`decodeTile`): widths from `width`, else `lanes` x 3.4-3.7 m + shoulders, else per class (residential 10 m, primary 16, footway 2...); building heights from `height`, else levels x 3.1 + 1.2-1.5, else per kind (house 6.5, apartments 11, retail 7...), else by footprint size. Sidewalks only where tagged (`sidewalk=*`); separate sidewalks are drawn as the footways they are.
- **Terrain:** AWS Open Data Terrain Tiles (terrarium z15, USGS 3DEP), decoded by `data/terrain.js` (a 70-line PNG reader with an injected `inflate`), bilinear between pixel centres across tile edges.
- **Valencia prebuilt:** `node tools/roam/build-town.mjs valencia` asks Overpass for 16 boxes of ~2.7 km (sequential, 20 s apart, our User-Agent, 60 s back-off on 429/503/504; ~6 minutes; cached in `tools/roam/cache/`, not committed), fetches 144 terrain PNGs, and writes `client/public/roam/valencia/16/<x>/<y>.json` + `index.json` (base elevation 342.1 m at the origin). Box: 34.375-34.475 N, 118.63-118.50 W (Westridge and the coasters to Bouquet Canyon, Copper Hill to Newhall). **576 tiles, 7.4 MB** raw (biggest 37 KB): 40,345 buildings, 22,079 road pieces, 3,367 areas, 1,185 trees, 878 named places. OSM base 2026-10-09. Not precached by the service worker (`vite.config.js onDemand '/roam/'`); a visited tile is kept by the service worker like any file.
- **Street nodes for a prebuilt town:** `node tools/roam/add-street-nodes.mjs valencia` (one Overpass query for the box, cached in `tools/roam/cache/<town>/street.json`; `build-town.mjs` reads the same cache, so a rebuild keeps them) writes `s` into the existing tiles. Valencia (2026-10-09): **41 lamps, 575 signals, 617 stop signs** in 227 tiles; the set is 7.42 MB.
- **Piers** (`man_made=pier`, now in the town query and `KEEP_TAGS`): a pier mapped as a line is a footway deck (`F.bridge`; 6 m wide if named, else a 2.5 m boat dock), level at its land end's height; a pier mapped as an area (the Newport and Balboa piers) is a **platform** (tile `q: [[own, height dm, name, ...ring]]`, kept by every tile it reaches, drawn by the tile its middle is in): a level deck at the middle height of its corners on dry land (at least 2 m), boards on pilings with a railing along the sides over the water (`render/linework.js platformArrays`); `deckSurfaces` and `deckAt` stand you on it, and what's on it (a building, an egg) stands on the deck. Its name is a place (`pier` POI) for the eggs.
- **Anywhere else:** `client/api/town.js` (Vercel function, `?z=16&x&y`): Overpass for the tile + 40 m (overpass-api.de, z., maps.mail.ru; 25 s each), the terrain PNG(s), `buildTile`, `Cache-Control: s-maxage=2592000` (30 days on Vercel's CDN). Mounted in `vite.config.js` for dev. Measured locally: a Torrance tile in 3.3 s (90 roads, 37 buildings, 25 areas). The browser asks `/roam/<town>/...` for tiles inside the index's box and `/api/town` outside it (`stream.js`): up to 3 static and 1 function request at a time, nearest first, 2 s -> 60 s pause after a failure, 60 decoded tiles kept.

## The coast (Newport Beach)
- **The sea from the coastline** (`data/sea.js`): OSM's coast is lines with the land on their left (`natural=coastline`), never a polygon. Per tile (and once for the whole town), the lines are joined end to start, clipped to the box and each piece leaving the box is joined to the next one coming in by walking clockwise round the box's edge; closed rings inside the box are islands; the rings are read even-odd. A tile no coastline crosses is all sea or all land: `build-town.mjs` asks the town-wide sea (`townSea`, over every tile the box touches), `/api/town` says sea when nothing is mapped on the tile and the terrain is 5 m under sea level.
- **Bays and the harbour:** Newport's coastline crosses the harbour mouth; the harbour is `natural=bay` "Newport Bay" (its 14 islands are inner rings). On a coast, bays, `water=bay|harbour|lagoon`, tidal water and any water whose shore is under 1.5 m are water at sea level too (tile `wb: [[ring, ...], ...]`, each group even-odd). Decoded: `tile.sea` (groups), `tile.shore` (ring edges that aren't the clip box and have land on one side), `tile.seaY` (sea level in the town's frame), `tileSeaAt(t, x, z)`.
- **The ground:** under the water the lattice is at least 1.5 m down (0.3 m within half a cell of land, so the slope between the last land point and the water doesn't dip under the water's level); land beside it at least 0.4 m up. The ground under the water is painted a sea-floor teal.
- **The water** (`render/sea.js`): one shared ShaderMaterial, a mesh per tile (the rings triangulated with their holes) at sea level, near and far: three travelling swells from the south-west plus a fine chop near you make the normal (no textures), Fresnel sky reflection (the sky colour the town uses), the sun's glitter, deep blue-green, a surf strip along every shore edge with foam that breaks and runs back, dimmed at night, fogged. One draw call a sea tile.
- **You and the water:** `shore` edges are walls (a second collider set, `createColliders().addEdges`) for walking and driving; you walk the sand to the water's edge, never into the sea (if a step would land in the sea from dry land you stay put). Out on a pier you're above it: its sides are railings (a step off the deck over the water is refused). Swimming and boats come with the water phase.
- **Numbers:** 136 of Newport's 323 tiles have sea. Walking the 300 m of the Newport Pier stays on the deck end to end; stepping off its side over the water is refused; walking down the beach stops at the water's edge, never under sea level (`s3.mjs`).

## Going to another town
- Menu ☰ > **Go to...** lists the other towns (`RoamMenu towns/onGo`, under the simplicity rule a fold-out). Pickleball.jsx `hopTo(town)` shows a short card ("On the way to Simi Valley..."; at least 0.9 s), disposes the world and starts the new town at its venue's spot (`roamUi.from` = that venue, so **Back to the courts** goes there).
- **Driving, you arrive in your car** (`world.arriveByCar({ model, color })`: the nearest through road to the town's spot, its right-hand lane, facing along it). On foot you arrive on foot.
- **Riders come too** (`server/roam` `roam:hop { town }`): the driver's riders get `roam:hop { town, driver }` (their page follows: "Riding along to..."), and a **ticket** (30 s, memory only) that puts them in the driver's instance when either joins, and in the passenger seat (`roam:seat { num, car }`) once the driver's car is there. A ticket that runs out: the rider just arrives on foot.
- Measured (`s2.mjs`, two phones): Ava drives, Ben rides along, Ava picks Simi Valley in the menu: both in Simi Valley in the same instance, Ava driving, Ben in her car (0.0 m), Ava drives 31 m and Ben comes along.

## Rendering and the phone budget
- **Near tiles** (380 m phone / 560 m desktop): the ground (the lattice, its texture painted per tile: 512 px phone / 1024 desktop, land use, parks, water, lots, sidewalks and roads underneath, **a lawn round every mapped house and a concrete apron round other buildings**), **road surfaces as geometry** (8 m pieces draped on the ground, round joins, sidewalks where mapped, **curbs and gutters** along mapped sidewalks), lane markings (double yellow on two-way arterials, white lane dashes, edge lines on freeways), **stall lines** in mapped lots (the grid the parked cars use, `sim/parked.js lotStalls`), bridge decks with parapets, buildings (see Materials), mapped trees, parked cars, the map's signals/stop signs/lamps, wall colliders.
- **Far tiles** (to 900 m phone / 1,500 desktop / 700 on Low): ground (128/256 px) and plain blocks (sheds skipped). Fog to the edge; the Real Sky dome rides with the camera.
- **Materials** (`render/tilemesh.js`, Medium/High): the venues' CC0 ambientCG textures (lent by the host, `park/surfaces.js`) multiply each surface's own hashed paint, sampled **along each wall** (metres along it, metres up) and **up each roof slope** (`win` = u, v, style, surface; `render/buildings.js SURF`): stucco walls, concrete tilt-up panels with a joint every 9 m on works, warehouses and big boxes, clay tile or (1 in 4 houses) lighter composition shingle roofs, gravel/membrane flat roofs; the grain's normal lights it a little (relief); detail fades out past 40-160 m. The ground picks grass/asphalt/concrete grain from its own painted colour, plus a slow variation (lawns uneven, lots worn). Houses get **eave shade** on the top 0.6 m of their walls, a **white fascia** and a **soffit** under the overhang. **Windows** (shader, from the wall's metres; anti-aliased with `fwidth`): houses: wide framed windows with a mullion, a storey apart; shops: storefront glass (mullions every 1.6 m, now and then a door) under a darker fascia band, punched windows upstairs; tall buildings by a per-building hash: punched windows with balcony shade (apartments, hotels), ribbon bands (offices) or a curtain wall, a glass lobby on the ground floor; parking structures: open decks between parapets and columns. Glass shows a dark room (curtains here and there) and reflects the sky at a low angle (Fresnel); about half the windows are lit at night. Low: no textures (the phase 1 look).
- **Fixed in this round:** half of all houses had black roofs in phase 1 (a palette index from a signed shift went negative); `pick` now wraps and the hashes use unsigned shifts.
- **Sun shadows** (Medium/High): the sun casts shadows in a box round you (75 m phone / 120 m desktop; 1024/2048 map), snapped to its texels; buildings, decks, trees, parked cars and signal poles cast, everything receives. The map is redrawn only when you've moved 8 m, the tiles or the light changed, and every 2.5 s (a single redraw was sometimes lost in the tests, so each redraw is repeated on the next two frames). Your car and the passing cars keep a soft contact shadow (an instanced blob).
- **Trees:** the map's trees (and tree rows, and woods) only. Within 150 m (phone) / 240 m (desktop) they're the venues' trees (crossed leaf cards that sway, fan palms with skirts; kind 1 spots are palms, mostly fan palms), farther off a cheap trunk and crown. Valencia's map has 1,185 mapped trees, so most streets have none: nothing is scattered (the rule).
- **Light:** the town's bounce light is pavement and stucco (the hemisphere's ground colour pulled toward a warm grey, so shaded walls don't turn green) and the exposure is 0.9x the venues' (pale stucco in full sun read washed out).
- **Draw calls:** a near tile 3-5, a far tile 2; trees 4 plain + 2-3 per kit kind; parked and passing cars 2 per model plus 1 for all their shadows; street furniture 10. Measured 55-70 tile draws in town (phone).
- **Building:** one tile mesh a frame (two before any are up), near first, nearest first, the ring ahead of a car first.

## Cars (`render/carmodel.js`, `render/cars.js`)
- **Modelled in code** from real class sizes (no files; nothing downloaded): a mid-size sedan, a compact hatchback, a mid-size SUV, a full-size pickup (a covered bed) and the Turbo 98. A body lofted through ~40 rounded cross-sections (rocker, door bulge, beltline, a narrower glasshouse with tumblehome, a crowned roof), the plan view rounded at the ends, **wheel arches** cut into the sides, glass a little proud of the body with the pillars left in paint, headlights in black bezels with a projector, tail lights that wrap round the corners, amber side markers, a grille and intake, plain plates, a dark lower lip, mirrors; **wheels** (a rounded tire, a five-spoke rim) as their own part. ~2,300 triangles a car. The free CC0 kits (Kenney Car Kit 3.1, rgsdev's pack) were checked and not used: toy proportions (`client/src/roam/CREDITS.md`).
- **Shading:** one glossy MeshStandard for paint and glass (glass rides in it as near-black vertices: black times any colour stays glass), reflecting the host's HDRI (dimmed at night); trim in Lambert with a `glow` attribute (1 head, 2 tail) that lights at night (and the brake lights when you brake). Creased normals (36 degrees): smooth curves, crisp edges.
- **Parked and passing cars** are instanced (paint + trim with the wheels merged in); the nearest 70 (phone) / 160 (desktop) parked cars are drawn. **Your car and friends' cars** are meshes whose front wheels steer and every wheel rolls; yours leans out of turns and dips under the brake.
- **Fallback:** if a shape can't be built, the phase 1 boxy shape stands in (`fallbackGeometry`; tested).
- **Parked cars** now spread over a whole lot (at most 40% of its stalls, 48 a lot, 110 a tile) instead of filling its first rows; curb parking sits 1.0 m in from the curb (clear of the traffic lane).

## The chase camera (`sim/chase.js`)
Behind and above the car (6.0 m landscape / 6.9 m upright, plus up to 2.6 m with speed; 1.95-2.25 m up), **looking at the road ahead** (9-26 m ahead, a little above it) instead of the roof, so the hills tilt the view; it swings round after the car faster the faster you go, widens its field of view with speed (56 -> 67 degrees landscape, 66 -> 77 upright), stays out of the hill behind you and is pulled in by walls. No view offset in a car (the car sits in the lower third by itself). Tested.

## Life: traffic and people (`sim/traffic.js`, `sim/peds.js`)
- **Traffic:** 6 cars on a phone, 14 on a desktop (3 on Low), spawned 70-260 m from you and dropped past 330 m. Each drives a mapped road in its lane (US: right of the middle; two-way streets clear of curb parking) at the road's speed (11 m/s residential to 29 motorway; slower into bends), follows on at a way's end (a road meeting it within 4 m, mostly straight on, one-ways only their way), **stops at the map's stop signs** (a 1.6 s pause) and **red lights** (a 52 s cycle split by heading: never green both ways), keeps its distance and **never hits you**: anyone in its lane within 28 m ahead (you walking, your car's three points, friends) and it brakes to a stop well short and waits. Your car and you bump into it (its three circles join the colliders). Drawn instanced like parked cars.
- **People walking:** 3 on a phone, 6 on a desktop (none on Low), only on mapped footways, pedestrian streets and mapped sidewalks within 60 m of a shop (a retail/commercial/supermarket building or a shop/amenity place), spawned 25-110 m away; they walk up and down at 1.1-1.5 m/s, pause now and then, turn round at the ends, and wait if you're in the way. Figures only within 75 m (phone) / 120 m, animated without motion matching and every other frame on a phone.

## Street furniture and night (`render/street.js`)
- Where the map has them only: a **signal** pole at the corner (back from the cross street, out past the curb, the first of the four corners off every road), a mast arm over the lanes and two heads whose red/amber/green follow the same cycle as the traffic; a **stop sign** at the curb facing the traffic coming up to the corner (grey back); a **lamp post** (moved to the curb if mapped on the lanes) with its arm over the road. `streetFurniture` is pure and tested (never on a road).
- **Night** (from the true sun: 0 above 4 degrees, 1 below -6): lamps glow and light a pool of road, every moving car's headlights glow and light the road ahead (yours farther), tail lights glow, windows light up, the paint's reflections dim. Lit roads without mapped lamps stay dark (no invented lamps).

## Controls (phone first)
- **On foot** (`sim/walker.js`, `ui/RoamHud.jsx`): upright, a drag in the lower 45% walks (the stick appears under the thumb and follows it past its ring); sideways, the left 42%; a drag anywhere else turns and tilts the view. 15% dead zone, walk 0.8-1.6 m/s, jog to 3.8, **Run** (a toggle, off when you let go) 6.8. Nothing moves you for you. The camera comes round behind you only when you walk away from it; walls pull it in; it looks a little past you so more of the town shows. Keys: WASD/arrows, Shift runs, Q turns, Enter/F/E acts.
- **Cars** (`sim/car.js`, drawn by `render/cars.js`): bicycle model with speed-sensitive steering (0.6 rad at walking pace to 0.075 at top speed), gas 5.8-10 m/s², brake 13, hold Brake stopped to back up (7 m/s), drag and slope (a hill holds you back), terrain under the four wheels (pitch, roll, a little air off crests), bridge decks; walls by three circles along the car, the car turning to run along the wall and losing speed by how head-on it was (never through it). No damage. **Someone in front: the car stops** (`personAhead`). Phone: drag on the left half to steer (70 px = full lock), hold **Gas** / **Brake** on the right. Keys: W/S/A/D. Get out under 2 m/s, anywhere; the car stays (this session, for everyone in the instance). Top speeds 27-30 m/s, the Turbo 98 42.
- **Parked cars** (`sim/parked.js`): from the tile key (mulberry32), the same in every browser: rows of stalls along each mapped lot's long side (spread over the lot: at most 40% of its stalls, 48 a lot), and now and then at residential curbs facing the traffic; never in or within 2.6 m of a building, on another road's lanes or within 4.6 m of another car; 110 a tile.

## Online together
- `server/roam` (memory only; nothing per account): instances of 16 per town; positions six ints (x, z, y dm; yaw /256; speed 0.1 m/s; ACT bits walk/sprint/drive/ride), sent 6/s walking, 8/s driving, every 2 s still; batches every 160 ms (400/1000 ms when the month's traffic meter is at 80/95%); 15 positions a second at most; car/ride 20 a minute; the last 200 left cars per instance; Ride along within 8 m of a driver, 3 riders. Measured (server test): two people driving 6 sends a second for a minute: under 40 KB a minute each received.
- Client (`sim/sync.js`): drawn 250 ms in the past with a glide past the last sample; remote cars as their own meshes; riders hidden in the car; names as page labels; riders get out when the driver does.
- Voice: `roam:vc` / `roam:sig` through `server/voice/relay.js` (room `t<n>`), the same session as My Park.

## How to add a town
1. `client/src/roam/towns/<id>.js`: `id, name, full, origin [lat, lon]` (a venue's origin if it opens from one: the frames match), `bbox`, `prebuilt: "/roam/<id>"` (or leave it out to stream everything from `/api/town`), `venues: { <venueId>: { x, z, yaw, back: { x, z, r } } }`, `spawn`, `eggs`, and `coast: true` on a coast (the sea from the coastline). Register it in `towns/index.js` (it then shows in Go to... too).
2. Add it to `server/roam/index.js TOWNS` (its reach in dm).
3. `node tools/roam/build-town.mjs <id>` (keep each town under ~10 MB, Valencia's 7.4 MB being the reference: trim the box, as Northridge's was; a dense downtown may need streaming only). Overpass may answer 429: the builder waits 60 s and goes on (about 8 minutes a town).
4. Eggs: pick real named features, run a spot finder (nearest open ground; see the test), write them with `place` set to the map's exact name; the test checks them.

## Rules kept
- Drawn from map data only (OSM + terrain): no invented buildings, trees, lamps or filler; eggs, cars, traffic and people walking are game items (on mapped roads and walks only). Textures, colours, windows, a lawn round a mapped house and an apron round other buildings are materials, not things. Business names only as map labels (the street/place names); no branded interiors.
- No real home or work addresses in the repo; Home/Work come later as private per-account pins.
- Nothing moves your player for you.
