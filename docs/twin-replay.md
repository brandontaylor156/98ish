# Twin Replay (Pickleball 98): a real game, filmed, replayed by the athletes

Built 2026-10-06 overnight (the owner asked for something "GREAT, GROUNDBREAKING" and free). Inside Pickleball 98 (no program of its own): **Game > Twin Replay (film a real game)...** opens screen `"twin"`. Help: the "Twin Replay" section of the `pickleball` topic (games.js); privacy lines in support.js (IndexedDB `98ish-twin`, the jsDelivr/Google model download).

## What it does
1. **Capture**: film in-app (`Recorder.jsx`: back camera 1280x720 ideal, MediaRecorder mp4/H.264 on Safari else WebM, 2.5 Mbit/s, sound without echo cancellation, Wake Lock, max 45 min) or **Open a video**. The video goes to IndexedDB (`store.js`), never uploaded.
2. **Calibrate** (`Calibrate.jsx`): pick a moment (slider), tap the 4 court corners in order (near left, near right, far right, far left as seen from the camera; a little court picture lights the next one); a 3x magnifier follows the finger, dots can be dragged; court lines are drawn through the inverse homography to check; Doubles / Singles. Taps are stored in 0..1 video coordinates.
3. **Read** (`reader.js`):
   - **Frames**: `frameStepper` plays the (in-page, nearly invisible) `<video>` and pauses on each wanted frame via `requestVideoFrameCallback` (decoding in order: ~0.1 s a frame here vs ~0.6 s for seeking each frame); seeks only to start or without rVFC. Browser recordings without a duration (MediaRecorder WebM: `Infinity`) are fixed by a jump to the end first. Each frame is drawn at up to 1920 px (the crops' source) and 640 px (the analysis frame, colors).
   - **What the model sees** (`core/regions.js`): MediaPipe's detector finds people at roughly a quarter of its input's height or more, and fence-cam players are ~35-60 px in a 640 px frame, so it's never shown the whole picture: each tracked player gets a 256 px crop round where they're heading (`playerBox`: 1.9 m tall x 1.55 room at the local scale, IMAGE mode, 1 pose), and court tiles (`scanTiles`: squares about 3 player heights across, a far row and a near row, 30 % overlap; ~8 tiles) are scanned every 6th frame and whenever a player is missing (3 poses each). Duplicates merged by foot position (`mergePeople`). `courtRegion` bounds the tiles (no stands).
   - **Model**: MediaPipe **Pose Landmarker** lite (5.8 MB) in a module Worker (`poseWorker.js`; a synchronous `importScripts` stand-in because the WASM loader calls it and module workers forbid it); GPU delegate with an OffscreenCanvas, CPU fallback; if the GPU averages > 350 ms over the first 6 frames (a software GPU) it switches to the CPU; on the page if the worker can't start in 45 s.
   - Torso color histograms from the 640 px frame; the sound decoded with an OfflineAudioContext at 12 kHz mono. Pause / Stop / progress + ETA; a live top-down dot view of the tracks. `readStats` reports frames, ms per frame (frame step, model), inferences per frame, where/delegate, worker error.
4. **Analyze** (`core/`, pure, Node-tested):
   - `homography.js`: normalized DLT least squares (4+ points), inverse, convexity check (crossed corners refused), court lines in image space.
   - `tracker.js`: feet (heels/ankles/toes weighted by visibility, else hips + leg length) through H to court meters; people > 2.6 m outside the lines ignored; Hungarian assignment on predicted-position distance + a net-crossing penalty + 1.5 x color distance; one-euro smoothing; tracks born from the first frames that see them; `balanceTeams` (analyze.js) makes doubles 2 v 2 by mean depth.
   - `onsets.js`: band-pass ~3 kHz twice, 5 ms energy hops, local peaks 9 dB over a running median floor with a 4 dB rise and a brightness check (band vs full energy); 0.22 s minimum gap. `addPop` makes the synthetic pop.
   - `hits.js` `soundOffset`: the sound may be out of step with the picture (recorder start-up, Bluetooth): pops are shifted -0.6..+0.6 s and the shift where they best meet someone's swing peak (middle of the best plateau) is used if it scores 15 % better than none; stored as `soundOffset`. A loud, bright pop (>= 12 dB, brightness >= 0.2) is accepted with a weak swing (soft serves, small far players).
   - `hits.js`: wrist speed relative to the hips (3D world landmarks in m/s, else 2D scaled by body height), smoothed; each pop goes to the opposite team's player whose wrist peaked hardest within -0.28..+0.18 s (below 2.4 = bounce/noise, dropped); without sound, swing peaks alone with team alternation (the stronger of two same-team peaks within 0.6 s wins: wind-ups). Rallies split at 4 s gaps; incoming ball "bounced" for the first three hits, deep hitters (> 4.2 m) and low contacts; kinds: serve, return, overhead (> 1.85 m), lob (next hitter pushed deep, flight > 1.35 s), volley (no bounce at the kitchen), dink (at the kitchen), drop (to the kitchen, slow, low), drive. Forehand/backhand from the swinging wrist's side (team 0 faces away from the camera).
   - `ballpath.js`: the ball is **rebuilt**, not tracked: contact points in front of/beside the hitter at the measured hand height; ballistic arcs (no drag) between hits; a bounce on the receiver's side (past the kitchen for serves) placed by searching the time fraction closest to a per-shot preference that clears the net (0.89 m + 4 cm) and leaves upward; the last shot lands deep.
   - `stats.js`: per-player heat map (6 x 8 cells of their half), kitchen-line time (depth 1.68-3.03 m) during rallies, distance (tracking jumps ignored), shot mix, third shots, rally-ending shots, the returner's depth on their serves; rally-length histogram; highlights (longest, then fastest exchanges).
   - `replay.js`: 30 fps engine frames: per player the situation anim.js wants (x, z, v, facing, ball, prep 0.65 s before each of their hits, swing 0.75 s after, oppHit for split steps, mate/across, phase); the ball in the server's hand before the serve; `events` (hit/bounce sounds) on frames.
   - `twinfile.js`: the share file: players' paths on a 10 Hz grid in delta-coded cm, hits as arrays, venue/court/title; gzip in the browser; validated on open (bounded sizes, clamped values); ~7 KB for a 9-shot rally.
5. **Replay** (`TwinPlayer.jsx` + engine hook): rally by rally on Pickleball 98's athletes at a venue: the owner's real venues on a chosen court (`venues.js`: My Park's `loadVenueSpec` + `makeLayout` + `buildCourtVenue`) or the game venues. Controls: play/pause, prev/next rally (auto-advance option), scrubber with hit ticks, 1x/½x/¼x, cameras Broadcast/Side/Top/Follow (pick a player)/Fence; More options: the original video beside it in sync (picture-in-picture), venue and court, Send to a friend (Web Share / download of the `.twin` file), Delete. Stats tab with the cards above.
6. **Demo** (`demo.js`): the synthetic rally through the real pipeline (More options > Try the demo rally).

## Engine hook (engine.js)
`playTwin({ frames, venue, roster })` starts a held local match with the roster and an external replay (`replay.external`); `twinControl({ paused, speed, cam, follow })`, `twinSeek(seconds)`, `twinState()`, `stopTwin()`. External replays don't end on taps (`endReplay(force)`), advance at `speed` (`stepTwin`), stop on the last frame (`twinEnd` event), place figures on seek (`placeTwin`: new anims, no slide), play hit/bounce sounds from frame events and split steps for the other team. Cameras in `twinCamera`. Pickleball.jsx: lazy `TwinReplay` for `screen === "twin"`, a Game-menu item, and `getEngine`; TwinReplay.css hides the match's own overlays on that screen (`.pkStage[data-screen="twin"] > :not(.pkCanvas):not(.pkTwin)`).

## Tests
- `node --test client/src/components/applets/pickleball/twin/twin.test.js` (8; incl. the late-sound offset and the crop regions): homography (exact, projection recovered within 1 cm, least squares, crossed corners refused), tracking (Hungarian optimal, one-euro, identities through shuffled detections: purity > 97 %, max error < 0.5 m), onsets (5 pops found within 20 ms among footsteps and a bounce), shot kinds, ball path (net clearance, bounce sides, contacts met, volley = one arc), the synthetic fence-cam end to end (positions < 0.5 m, all 9 hits within 0.15 s with the right team, serve/return/dink/volley, stats; without sound >= 8/9 hits), replay frames + share file round trip.
- `synthetic.js`: a pinhole fence camera, a scripted 9-shot doubles rally (serve, return, drop, dinks, volleys) with eased movement, 33-landmark bodies (pixels + world), swings peaking ~8 m/s, a soundtrack with pops, bounces, footsteps and hiss.
- Browser (scratchpad `twin/`, vite 5215, `run.sh` takes the shared Chrome lock): `tw-demo.mjs desktop|phone` (Game menu > Twin Replay > demo: plays, camera cycles, pause holds, seek, Stats cards, no sideways scroll on the phone, leaving stops the replay, no page errors); `tw-e2e.mjs` (the engine renders the scripted rally from the fence camera, recorded with pops; the real reader + MediaPipe reads that video; positions and hits compared with the script; results below).

## Measured (2026-10-06, this machine: headless Chrome, CPU WebAssembly, other agents running)
- **Synthetic landmarks** (unit test, no model): every player within 0.5 m all the way, identity purity > 97 %, all 9 hits within 0.15 s with sound (and with the sound 0.4 s late: offset found within 0.05 s), 8/9 from swings alone.
- **Engine-rendered video through the whole browser pipeline** (`tw-rec.mjs` records the scripted rally from the fence camera at the stadium, crowd and umpire included; `tw-read.mjs` reads it with MediaPipe): 4/4 players tracked, identity purity 0.94-1.00, court position error median 0.38-0.52 m, p90 0.67-0.76 m (the recording's own timing map and the athletes' animation smoothing are part of that). Hits: 7 of 9 found, each 0.143-0.155 s after the true pop (a constant lag; the sound offset was ~0.41 s in the recording and was found as ~0.56 s); the serve and the return were missed (the replayed serve barely moves the wrist). Earlier, before the crops, the model saw 0.2 people per frame and tracked 0-2 players.
- **Speed**: frame step ~0.10 s (playback stepping; seeking was ~0.6 s), model ~0.5-0.65 s per frame for ~5.8 crops (CPU WASM in headless Chrome under load) → ~1.3 frames/s. A real phone's GPU should be several times faster; a 10-minute game at 15 fps is 9,000 frames, so expect a long read on a phone (the ETA shows it). Lowering `READ_FPS` to 10 or reading only the rallies are the next speed-ups.
- **Software GPU**: headless Chrome's GPU delegate ran at 0.07-0.4 frames/s; the auto-switch to the CPU handles that.

## Left / honest limits
- Not tried on a real iPhone or a real filmed game end to end (only the engine-rendered video and the synthetic data). The first real game is the test that matters: tell the owner to film one rally with the phone on the back fence.
- The ball is rebuilt, not followed; points/errors aren't judged; shot kinds are heuristics.
- Seeking a video frame by frame is the slow part on phones; WebCodecs decoding would be faster (later).
- Occlusion (a player behind their partner) is bridged by prediction for up to ~1 s; longer gaps may swap names.
- Not linked to Real Games / Pickleball Club matches yet (scores aren't read from the video).
